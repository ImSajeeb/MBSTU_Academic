*Reference codes: Q1(a), Q1(c), Q4(c), Q20(a), Q25(a), Q25(b), Q25(d)*

**Master Answer**

**English Answer**

**Ethics, law, and professional responsibility** are related but they are not the same thing.

**Ethics** refers to moral principles that help us decide what is right or wrong. An action may be unethical even when it is not clearly illegal. For example, using a user\'s private information for advertising without properly informing the user may be ethically wrong.

**Law** refers to rules made and enforced by the government. Laws define what people and organizations are legally allowed or required to do. Breaking a law may result in legal punishment, fines, or other consequences. For example, collecting or sharing personal data in violation of applicable privacy laws can create legal problems.

**Professional responsibility** means the duties that a professional has toward users, employers, colleagues, and society. A software engineer has a responsibility to develop reliable and secure software, protect privacy, be honest about risks, and avoid unnecessary harm.

These three concepts can sometimes lead to different decisions. For example, a company may legally be able to collect certain data, but an engineer may still believe that using the data without clear user understanding is unethical. Professional responsibility may also require the engineer to raise the concern with management.

In software engineering, professional responsibility includes **public safety, privacy, security, honesty, competence, quality, fairness, and respect for users**. Engineers should not think only about the company\'s profit. They should also consider how their software can affect people and society.

When organizational goals conflict with public interest, a professional should identify the risk, communicate it clearly, suggest safer alternatives, document important concerns, and use appropriate professional or organizational channels if necessary.

*Reference codes: Q2(a), Q2(b), Q20(b), Q24(b), Q25(c)*

**Master Answer**

**English Answer**

There are three important ethical frameworks that can help a software engineer make decisions: **Utilitarianism, Deontology, and Virtue Ethics**.

**1. Utilitarianism**

Utilitarianism judges an action mainly by its consequences. The ethically better action is the one that produces the greatest overall benefit and the least overall harm for all affected people.

For example, if an AI system collects additional user data, the company may argue that it will improve the service for millions of users. A utilitarian analysis would compare this benefit with possible harms such as privacy loss, misuse of data, discrimination, or security risks. If the overall harm is greater than the benefit, the action should not be taken.

**2. Deontology**

Deontology focuses on **duties, rules, rights, and principles**, rather than only consequences. A person should do the right thing because it is their duty.

For example, collecting private messages without proper consent may be wrong even if it increases company profit. Users have a right to privacy, and the engineer has a duty to respect that right.

**3. Virtue Ethics**

Virtue Ethics focuses on the **character of the person making the decision**. A good professional should have qualities such as honesty, fairness, responsibility, courage, respect, and integrity.

For example, an engineer who discovers a serious security problem should not hide it just because management wants a quick release. A responsible and honest engineer would report the problem and try to protect users.

**Comparison**

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Framework**    **Main focus**      **Basic question**
  ---------------- ------------------- --------------------------------------------------------
  Utilitarianism   Consequences        Which action creates the most overall benefit?

  Deontology       Duties and rights   What is the right thing to do according to our duties?

  Virtue Ethics    Character           What would a good and responsible professional do?
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In real software engineering decisions, these frameworks can be considered together. An ethical decision should try to reduce harm, respect people\'s rights, and reflect good professional character.

**সহজ বাংলা**

**Utilitarianism** ফলাফলের উপর গুরুত্ব দেয়। যে সিদ্ধান্তে সবচেয়ে বেশি মানুষের উপকার এবং সবচেয়ে কম ক্ষতি হবে, সেটিকে ভালো সিদ্ধান্ত ধরা হয়।

**Deontology** duty, rule এবং rights-এর উপর গুরুত্ব দেয়। কোনো কাজের ফল ভালো হলেও যদি কাজটি মানুষের অধিকার লঙ্ঘন করে, তাহলে সেটি ভুল হতে পারে।

**Virtue Ethics** মানুষের character-এর উপর গুরুত্ব দেয়। একজন ভালো software engineer honest, fair, responsible, courageous এবং respectful হবে।

সহজভাবে মনে রাখার জন্য:

**Utilitarianism → Result**\
**Deontology → Duty/Right**\
**Virtue Ethics → Character**

**Master Answer**

**English Answer**

Privacy is an important ethical responsibility in computing because software systems can collect large amounts of personal information. Personal data may include location, health information, browsing history, messages, contacts, biometric information, or workplace activity.

**Informed consent** means that a person agrees to data collection after receiving enough clear information about what data will be collected, why it will be collected, how it will be used, and who may receive it.

Simply clicking **"Accept"** on a long privacy policy does not always mean that meaningful informed consent has been obtained. If important information is hidden in complicated language, the user may not properly understand the consequences.

Ethical data collection should follow several principles:

1.  **Clarity:** Information should be explained in simple language.

2.  **Transparency:** Users should know what data is collected and how it is used.

3.  **Voluntariness:** Users should not be unfairly forced to provide unnecessary information.

4.  **Specificity:** Consent should clearly relate to specific purposes.

5.  **Purpose limitation:** Data should be used for the stated purpose.

6.  **Data minimization:** Only necessary data should be collected.

7.  **Security:** Collected information should be protected from unauthorized access.

8.  **Accountability:** Organizations should be responsible for how they collect and use data.

A better consent process would clearly explain important data practices before consent. For example, a mobile application could separately state: **"This application continuously collects precise location data and may share it with selected third parties."** Users should be given meaningful choices where appropriate.

Privacy is especially important when data can reveal sensitive information about people\'s lives. Even when data is collected for a useful purpose, such as public-health research, the organization should reduce privacy risks through anonymization or de-identification, limited access, security controls, and restricted use.

**Master Answer**

**English Answer**

Privacy and social benefit can sometimes come into conflict. A technology may provide significant benefits to society while also creating privacy risks.

For example, public-health researchers may want to analyze mobile-phone location data to understand the movement of people during a disease outbreak. Such analysis may help authorities respond quickly and save lives. However, location data can also reveal where individuals live, work, study, and travel.

The ethical principle **"Contribute to society and human well-being"** supports using technology for valuable social purposes. At the same time, **"Respect privacy"** requires professionals to protect individuals from unnecessary collection or use of personal information.

The best approach is to balance the two goals. Before collecting data, professionals should ask:

-   Is the data really necessary?

-   Is there a less intrusive alternative?

-   Can the data be anonymized?

-   Can access be restricted?

-   How long should the data be stored?

-   Who will be responsible for protecting it?

Useful safeguards include **data minimization, anonymization, encryption, access control, limited retention, informed consent where appropriate, transparency, independent review, and accountability**.

Therefore, social benefit should not automatically justify unlimited access to personal data. The method of achieving the benefit should also respect people\'s rights and privacy.

If an AI system works as a black box, affected people may not understand why a decision was made. This can create several societal harms.

First, it can cause **unfair treatment and discrimination**. If a system rejects applicants from a particular community, it may be difficult to identify whether the system is using unfair patterns.

-   The general purpose of the system

-   Important factors considered in the decision

-   The result of the decision

-   Important limitations of the system

-   How to request human review

-   How to challenge or appeal the decision

Explainability does not always mean revealing the complete source code. The main goal is to provide enough meaningful information for people to understand, question, and fairly challenge important decisions.

**Master Answer**

**English Answer**

**Algorithmic bias** occurs when an algorithm or AI system produces systematically unfair results for certain individuals or groups.

Bias can arise even when developers do not intentionally discriminate. There are several possible causes.

**1. Biased Training Data**

If training data reflects existing social bias, the AI may learn and reproduce that bias.

**2. Historical Discrimination**

Past discrimination in areas such as employment or lending can become part of the data. An AI trained on such data may continue the same pattern.

**3. Proxy Variables**

A system may not directly use a sensitive variable such as race or gender, but other variables can act as proxies. For example, location may indirectly represent socioeconomic or demographic characteristics.

**4. Sampling Problems**

If some groups are underrepresented in the training data, the model may perform poorly for those groups.

**5. Inappropriate Model Objectives**

A system designed only to maximize prediction accuracy, profit, engagement, or speed may ignore fairness and produce harmful outcomes.

Bias can be detected by comparing model performance and outcomes across different groups. Developers can examine measures such as selection rates, error rates, false-positive rates, false-negative rates, and other appropriate fairness measures.

To improve fairness, organizations can:

-   Improve the quality and diversity of training data.

-   Test models on different demographic groups.

-   Check for proxy variables.

-   Use suitable fairness techniques.

-   Perform regular audits.

-   Include diverse teams in development.

-   Keep human oversight for important decisions.

-   Provide an appeal or review process.

-   Monitor the system after deployment.

Simply removing an explicit demographic variable does not always remove discrimination. Other variables may contain similar information and act as proxies. Therefore, fairness must be evaluated based on the **actual outcomes and system behavior**, not only on which variables are included.

-   **Potential conflict:** A situation could develop into a conflict in the future.

-   **Perceived conflict:** Other people may reasonably believe that the person\'s judgment is influenced, even if it is not actually influenced.

একজন ethical software engineer শুধু **"system বানানো সম্ভব কি?"** সেটা দেখবে না। সে দেখবে **"এই অবস্থায় system launch করা উচিত কি না?"**

**Privacy → Consent → Security → Fairness → Explainability → Accountability → IP → Professional responsibility → Public interest**

**Q25. Comprehensive Case: The Ethical Software Engineer**

The platform uses personal data to make recommendations, contains a machine-learning model that has shown possible demographic bias, and depends on several third-party open-source components. During final testing, your team also discovers a serious security vulnerability. Management asks the team to proceed with the launch because the product has already been publicly announced.*

**(k)** Provide a justified final recommendation explaining whether the system should be launched immediately, delayed for remediation, or subjected to additional independent review before deployment.

*Reference codes: Q20(d), Q21(e), Q22(b--d), Q24(e), Q25(k)*

**Master Answer**

**English Answer**

If a software system has serious unresolved security vulnerabilities, significant privacy risks, or substantial unexplained fairness problems, I would **not recommend immediate deployment**.

The better approach would be to **delay the launch until the most serious risks are addressed**. If the system is extremely important or complex, an independent security, privacy, or fairness review may also be appropriate before deployment.

The reason is simple: the fact that a product has already been announced does not remove the professional responsibility to protect users and the public.

The organization should first fix or mitigate serious security vulnerabilities, review data collection and consent practices, test the AI system for bias, improve transparency and explainability, verify third-party licenses, and establish monitoring and incident-response procedures.

After these steps, the organization can make a more informed deployment decision.

A responsible engineer should prefer a **safe and justified delay over an immediate launch that creates serious and avoidable harm**.

**সহজ বাংলা**

যদি system-এ serious security vulnerability, বড় privacy risk বা serious unexplained bias থাকে, তাহলে **immediately launch করা উচিত নয়**।

প্রথমে risk কমাতে হবে, প্রয়োজন হলে independent review করতে হবে। Product আগে announce করা হয়েছে---এটা user-এর safety এবং engineer-এর professional responsibility-এর চেয়ে বড় নয়।

তাই সবচেয়ে responsible decision হলো:

**Fix/mitigate → Review → Test → Safeguard → তারপর deployment**
